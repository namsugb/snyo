# Typography

## Font Direction

- The overall type system should combine readability with a handwritten wedding feel.
- Use a clean, highly readable body font for invitation details and long text.
- Use a handwriting-style or calligraphy-style accent font selectively for names, section titles, short emotional phrases, or decorative emphasis.
- Decorative type should feel soft and personal, not theatrical or hard to read.

## Hierarchy

- Display: couple names, hero title, or key romantic headline
- Heading 1: major section titles such as greeting, gallery, location, or photo upload
- Heading 2: subsection titles or highlighted information blocks
- Body: invitation text, schedule, location details, upload guidance
- Caption: photo notes, dates, small labels, or supporting metadata

## Usage Rules

- Keep mobile readability as the first priority.
- Reserve handwriting-style typography for emphasis and atmosphere, not entire paragraphs.
- Make the wedding date, couple names, and venue details immediately legible.
- Keep line lengths short enough for comfortable reading on small screens.
- Avoid mixing too many font personalities in a single screen.

## Motion-Linked Typography

- Headline or decorative text may use a subtle alternating animation inspired by `movingimage1.png` and `movingimage2.png`.
- The motion should feel like a gentle living illustration or slightly shifting printed frame.
- Use this effect sparingly on high-impact elements only.
